Technology and capital are compounding faster than any institution can steer them. GTC is Gitcoin's governance token, and the name describes what governance is for: helping steer that technocapital toward what matters, the people, places and public goods markets won't fund on their own. The "capital" in the name is the world's, not the token holders'.

GTC is a governance token. It gives holders a voice in Gitcoin governance and no ownership of, and no right to the revenue, profits or assets of, Gitcoin, love.fund or Techne. Holding GTC is not an investment in any of them, and nothing on this page describes an expected return.

## The two ventures

Gitcoin's organizational entities are incubating two ventures for its next era. GTC holders don't own them, and their success or failure doesn't change what GTC is.

| Venture | What it is | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Techne](https://techne.computer/)** | An everything app for localism, and a movement for renegotiating our relationship with technology. Pilots so far: Beacon (local events) and Buoy (local money, where quadratic funding now lives). | Own accounts, brand and a planned 501(c)(3). Launch targeted for late 2027. |
| **love.fund** | Gitcoin's mainstream funding brand: pilots and campaigns that route money to the people and places we love, with crypto as plumbing rather than the pitch, disclosed wherever it's used. | Prototype at [app.love.fund](http://app.love.fund/) (play money so far). Launch targeted for February 14, 2027. |

The two are complementary: love.fund pushes crowdfunding forward, and Techne pushes forward consumer distribution. love.fund is where Techne and other aligned projects can raise, and Techne can bring distribution to love.fund's experiments. See [Ecosystem](/reference/ecosystem/).

## Governance structure

| Body | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **GTC holders and delegates** | Vote on proposals, directly or by delegating their voting power. |
| **[Governance forum](https://gov.gitcoin.co/)** | Where everything starts. Ideas go up as a temp check, then as a formal proposal. |
| **Snapshot** | Offchain, gasless votes on temp checks and proposals. Quorum is 1.5M GTC, lowered from 2.5M by a Snapshot vote that closed October 15, 2026. |
| **Onchain Governor + Timelock** | Binding onchain votes that execute through the timelock. In October 2026 the DAO upgraded to an OpenZeppelin v5 Governor, after a Snapshot vote in September and an onchain vote that closed October 1. It adds a Proposal Guardian that can cancel (never execute) malicious proposals, late-quorum protection and a DAO-adjustable quorum. |
| **Security Council** | An emergency safety backstop for governance and the treasury. |
| **Gitcoin Foundation** | A legal entity with its own board. It provides signers on the DAO's multisigs. |
| **Transitionary stewards** | A small, paid cohort (August 2026 to August 2027) stewarding Gitcoin's transition and holding the 3.0 team accountable. Budget payments release in roughly quarterly tranches, so each one is a checkpoint. |
| **[Trust graph](https://trustgraphs.xyz/)** | v1 went live September 15, 2026, in shadow mode first. The aim is to spread delegated voting power more widely over time, by rules anyone can inspect. |

**Supply.** The DAO can increase GTC supply by about 2% a year. It has never elected to do so.

## Future changes to governance utility

There is no GTC buyback or burn program. None has been proposed for a vote, none is planned, and nothing here should be read as a signal that one is coming.

The question came up in the [Binance hack thread](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/update-on-the-binance-hack-gtc/25448) (October 2026). One contributor's view there, not a decision by Gitcoin governance, was that any change to GTC's utility, including a buyback, shouldn't even be considered unless a proposal could show all five of these:

1. **Decentralized control.** No central party directs the network's success or GTC's token economics.
2. **Functional utility.** GTC has genuine utility independent of any expectation of buybacks.
3. **Transparent governance.** Funding, execution rules and oversight are all publicly disclosed.
4. **No promises of returns.** Buybacks are never marketed as yield or as guaranteed appreciation.
5. **Legal review.** Independent counsel assesses the program against applicable law before anything happens.

These are a minimum bar for discussion, not a roadmap. Whether any proposal could meet them is for independent counsel and Gitcoin governance to decide.
