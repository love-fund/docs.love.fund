These are areas we're interested in. They're ideas, not products: any experiment that holds or moves money, or touches investments or credit, goes through legal review before it ships.

## Frontburner

0. **QF and matching campaigns, Artizen or Gitcoin style.** This is the thing we know best. A sponsor puts up a matching pool, and small donations from many people decide how it gets split, so breadth of support counts for more than the size of any single check.
   - The experiment is in the packaging: shorter themed campaigns built around one place, cause or moment, instead of large open rounds.
   - Artizen-style mechanics are worth trying too, where backers get something back (an edition, an artifact or a membership) and that contribution feeds the match.
   - Each campaign is a test of the [pilot questions](/roadmap/wide-aperture-path/#pilots-short-term): who funds the pool, who shows up to give, and what changed when it was over.
1. **Supertake for impact.** Supertake already lets people follow and deploy into financial strategies, and that runs on existing rails like Coinbase and ERC-20s. We want the same flow for impact:
   1. Have a conversation with an AI about the impact you care about.
   2. It drafts an impact thesis for you.
   3. You publish the thesis, and others can follow or fork it.
   4. Donations made against the thesis flow into matching impact pools.
   5. Over time you see what your money did.
   - Supertake's financial strategies are its own product. An impact version would route donations, not investments, and promise no financial return.
   - The hard part is that impact has no equivalent of a token balance. Hypercerts might be the primitive, but few people are turning hypercerts into measured impact yet, so there's a cold-start problem to solve first.
2. **Trust graphs, or trust-based capital allocation.** Delegate funding decisions to people you trust, and let capital flow through those trust networks.
   - Most people don't have the time or context to evaluate every project. They do know whose judgment they trust, and a trust graph turns that into an allocation.
   - Open questions: how trust gets expressed (endorsements, vouches, past funding behavior), how far it carries before it decays, and how to keep the graph from turning into a clique.
   - It could be piloted with a community that already has dense relationships, such as greenpill.network or rebuild.how (RH).
3. **An atproto-native funding network.** It starts with a causes schema on atproto: a shared, open format for describing a cause, a project and a contribution, so funding data isn't locked inside love.fund.
   - From there it could grow into a newsfeed and social layer where you see what people you follow are funding and why, and give alongside them.
   - The idea is a funding network that funds relationships: money follows the social graph, and the graph gets stronger because money moves through it.
   - It also makes love.fund interoperable, so other atproto apps, techne among them, can read and write the same funding data.
