## Fund what you love, where you live. Bring us a pilot.

love.fund helps people fund the people and places they care about. We're looking for a few pilots to run with us. The pilot window is TBD.

### What you get

- **Matching money.** Per-pilot amount TBD, from a pool of TBD. The match is meant to go to the people and places you fund, not to fees or platform costs. Final pool terms are still being confirmed.
- **Software.** Donors give through love.fund. Nobody has to build a website or a payments system.
- **Our name and reach.** Your pilot gets presented as part of love.fund.
- **Operations.** We help write the rules page, run the match calculation, prepare payouts with our payments partner, and publish a plain "what your money did" readout.

### What we're looking for

- **A theme: TBD.** Our current default is localism: specific people and places.
- A community that already exists and will show up to give.
- One result we can all check at the end.
- Recipients we can pay through our payments partner. Eligible recipient types are TBD, pending the choice of payments partner and legal review.
- Bonus: other funders who will add money alongside the match.

### What we won't do

- Hold your money ourselves. Funds go through a regulated payments partner.
- Charge small donors.
- Use matching money for fees, salaries or platform costs.
- Change the rules once a pilot is live.

### How it works

1. Apply by the deadline (TBD). The form has 13 short questions and should take about 15 minutes.
2. We reply within a set number of business days (TBD). If we say no, you'll get the reason.
3. For pilots we back, we agree on the rules, dates and the result we'll check, and we write them down before launch.
4. The pilot runs. Payouts go out by the date in the rules.
5. We publish what happened, including what didn't work. Pilots that work can become ongoing love.fund campaigns.

### Plain disclosures

- Tax treatment depends on the payments partner and where you live. We expect gifts through a US nonprofit partner to be tax-deductible for US taxpayers. Outside the US, treat your gift as a donation, not a deduction. This is pending legal review.
- Donors fund work. They don't invest in it and get no financial return.
- If a pilot uses crypto, wallets or tokens, we'll say so plainly on its rules page.

## Application questions

We keep the form short. Every question is there because the screen uses it. We don't ask for budget spreadsheets, impact projections or team bios.

1. Pilot name and one sentence: who gets funded, where?
2. Who are you? Organization or person, website, and one thing you've delivered before (link).
3. Who receives the money? Type (US nonprofit, non-US nonprofit, informal group or individuals) and how many.
4. Who will give, and how will you reach them? Include numbers if you have them (list size, past donors, members).
5. Money: how much match do you request (up to the per-pilot cap, TBD)? Is anyone else putting money in? Name them and say whether it's committed in writing.
6. What's the one result we can check at the end, and how will we check it?
7. Why will this work here, and why is love.fund's help needed?
8. How should giving and matching work? For example: QF match, 1:1 match, all-or-nothing pledge, or something new.
9. Proposed dates (open, close) inside the pilot window.
10. Does any software, platform or person involved take a fee or revenue share? How much, and on what?
11. Any relationship with Gitcoin stewards or staff, or with love.fund?
12. Does anything involve crypto, wallets or tokens? (optional)
13. Links: past results, community, press. (optional)

Please don't include personal details about the people you'd fund. We'll ask for what's needed later, through our payments partner.

## How we screen

### Hard gates

If an application fails any of these, we decline it with a reason.

- **Money source.** The money comes from the love.fund match pool (if selected) and/or a co-funder you name. If a pilot can't answer this, it isn't ready.
- **Checkable result.** One outcome we can check at the end, plus how we'll check it.
- **No match money for fees.** Match dollars don't pay fees, platform revenue shares, operating costs or the applicant's own costs.
- **No custody.** love.fund never holds the funds. Money moves through a payments partner.
- **Named audience.** Who gives, applies or votes, and the channel that reaches them.
- **Theory of change.** Why this works, and why here.
- **Within the cap.** The match requested is at or under the per-pilot cap (TBD).
- **Payable recipients.** Recipients can be paid through our payments partner. Cases like individuals, wallets or unregistered groups go to legal review rather than being rejected outright.
- **Within the window.** The pilot can open and close inside the pilot window (TBD).
- **Disclosure.** You disclose any relationship with Gitcoin stewards or staff, or with love.fund, and any fee or revenue share your own platform takes.

### Scored criteria

Applications that pass the gates are scored 0 to 3 on each criterion. Weights are draft.

| Criterion | Draft weight | What scores high |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | 20% | Past giving or participation data from this audience |
| Money leverage | 20% | Committed co-funding at least equal to the match request, or a crowd target at least equal to the match |
| Outcome and theory of change | 15% | A checkable outcome, a plausible mechanism, and a prior run or outside evidence |
| Fit | 15% | Specific people and places; localism |
| Partner | 10% | Delivered a comparable round or project, with public, independently checkable evidence |
| Operating load | 10% | Runs on love.fund and our payments partner with few manual steps |
| Novelty | 10% | Tests one of our [experiments](/roadmap/experiments/) or answers an open question we're tracking |

Co-funding isn't required, but it scores well. The match pool is finite, and pilots that bring other funders show the model can grow.

### Decisions

- Each application is scored twice, independently. Big disagreements go to a person to decide rather than being averaged.
- Score bands decide the next step: back it, ask for something specific, or decline. The thresholds are TBD.
- Every "no" comes with a reason. If most rejections share one reason, we treat that as a problem with the form, not with the applicants.
