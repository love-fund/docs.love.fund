Every era, humanity has reinvented how resource allocation works from first principles. The new onchain AI era will be no different.

We can study the past design space of how we funded what matters, and learn a lot from it. Who gets what, who decides, and how people eat has been redesigned again and again over 12,000 years, each time with new tools, new money and new ways of grouping people.

See the living poster: **[resource allocation through history](https://resource.gitcoin.co/)**.

## Five eras of resource allocation

| Era | When | Money | How people grouped | Example mechanisms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hunter-gatherer** | Before 10,000 BCE | Barter, primitive currency | Bands (20–50), tribes (50–150) | Gift circles, potlatch, Ubuntu, Kula ring, mutual aid networks, redistribution feasts |
| **Agricultural** | ~10,000 BCE – 1700 CE | Commodities, metal coins, labor | Villages, cities, city-states, empires | Irrigation collectives, pasture commons, granaries, rotating savings (ROSCAs, susu, tontines), tithes, waqf endowments |
| **Industrial** | ~1700 – late 20th century | Coins, paper money, banknotes, checks, bonds | Cities, nations, global empires | Unions, guilds, cooperatives, credit unions, joint-stock companies, foundations, the welfare state |
| **Information** | Late 20th century – present | Fiat, digital payments, cryptocurrencies | Megacities, nations, supranational orgs, global networks | Venture capital, crowdfunding, participatory budgeting, microlending, open source, bounties and hackathons, impact investing |
| **Onchain** | Emerging | Crypto, tokenized assets, governance tokens, NFTs | DAOs, blockchain networks, virtual communities, bioregions | Quadratic funding, retro funding, DAOs, bonding curves, impact attestations, hypercerts, conviction voting, money streaming, deep funding |

Each era didn't replace the last. It layered on top. Rotating savings clubs, mutual aid and gift circles are still running today, alongside venture capital and quadratic funding.

## What the design space teaches us

- **Every mechanism is a tradeoff.** Each one is good at something and bad at something else. Gift circles strengthen bonds but don't scale to strangers. Quadratic funding reads community breadth but depends on identity. Retro funding pays for proven results but leaves newcomers to carry the upfront risk. There's no perfect mechanism, only a fit for a context.
- **The tools change what's possible.** Writing gave us ledgers, coins gave us markets, the internet gave us crowdfunding, and blockchains gave us programmable treasuries. AI agents are the next shift: allocation that can read context, relationships and impact at a scale no committee could.
- **The design space is a landscape.** Think of every possible rule or mechanism as a point on a flat grid, and how well everyone does under it as the height. Most of history has been a slow walk across that landscape. We can now search it deliberately, by remixing known mechanisms and testing new ones.
- **Some of the best points haven't been built yet.** Combinations like retroactive quadratic funding, or a bonding curve that prices attested impact instead of market demand, are a few small edits away from mechanisms we already know.
- **The oldest mechanisms are the most relational.** Many of the [sacred economy rails](/roadmap/opportunity/#rails-for-a-sacred-economy) we want to build, like gift, trust, commons and federation, were the default in earlier eras. The opportunity is to bring them back with the reach of modern tools.

## Ancient futures

The design space doesn't only point forward. Some of the most important mechanisms are the oldest ones: gift circles, mutual aid, commons, rotating savings, redistribution feasts, the ethic of Ubuntu ("I am because we are"). They kept people fed and held for thousands of years, long before banks, insurers or welfare states existed.

To borrow Helena Norberg-Hodge's phrase, the future we want looks more like an **ancient future**. Not a return to the past, but a chance to re-indigenize and re-relationalize ourselves: to remember that an economy is made of relationships, with each other and with the living world, and to rebuild it that way with the tools of this era. That's what a [sacred economy](/start-here/dialectics/sacred-global-economy/) is.

This matters now because the old systems are breaking down. Jobs are being automated faster than people can retrain, institutions people counted on are under strain, and the safety nets of the industrial era were built for a world that's going away. When they fray, what's left is what has always been there: community.

That's the opportunity. A sacred, relational economy is also a social safety net:

- **Mutual aid and gift circles** catch people when markets and states don't.
- **Commons and shared treasuries** give communities something they own together, that can't be taken away.
- **Trust networks and solid community relations** are what people actually lean on in a crisis.
- **Local and bioregional economies** keep value circulating close to home instead of leaking out.

These mechanisms are arriving at exactly the moment they're needed. The work is to build them now, with modern reach and onchain rails, so they're strong enough to hold people when the old systems can't.

## The onchain AI era

We're at the start of the next reinvention. Money is programmable, communities can form around a shared treasury in an afternoon, and AI agents can allocate capital through trusted relationships.
