title: Networks x Hierarchies
description: Open networks and clear hierarchies look like rival ways to organize. The best structures use each where it fits.
---

## The tension

A hierarchy organizes people through clear lines of authority: someone decides, roles are defined, accountability runs up and down. A network organizes people through connection: many nodes, no single center, coordination that emerges from the edges.

They look opposed. Hierarchies are fast, accountable and legible, but they can become rigid and concentrate power. Networks are adaptive, resilient and open, but they can become slow, diffuse and hard to steer. Most organizations swing between the two, centralizing when things feel chaotic and decentralizing when things feel stuck.

The question is whether you can get the strengths of both without the failure modes of either. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/start-here/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: hierarchy.** Some decisions need an owner. Clear roles and authority let a group act quickly, hold someone accountable and protect what matters when things go wrong.
- **Antithesis: network.** Knowledge and energy live at the edges. Open networks let people self-organize, try things in parallel and route around failure without asking permission.
- **Synthesis: heterarchy.** Many small, nested structures, each with clear ownership inside and open connection between. Authority is local and temporary, held by whoever is closest to the work, and the network ties it all together.

## What each side protects

| Pole      | What it protects                                 | What it becomes alone |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Hierarchy | Accountability, speed, coherence                 | Bureaucracy and capture |
| Network   | Agency, adaptability, resilience                 | Drift and tyranny of structurelessness |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume structure has to be one shape everywhere: either the whole thing is a pyramid or the whole thing is a mesh.

But structure can vary by scale and by task. A small team can have a clear lead while the teams themselves relate as peers. A decision can have an owner for this season and a different owner next season. Hierarchy works best small, local and accountable to the people below it. Networks work best for connecting many of those small units without a single point of control.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Subsidiarity.** Decide at the smallest level that can handle the decision. Escalate only what really needs it.
- **Clear owners, open edges.** Every project has someone accountable, and anyone can start a new project or join one.
- **Rotating and earned authority.** Leadership follows contribution and trust, and it can be handed on, rather than being fixed by title.
- **Fractal funding.** On love.fund, funding can flow through a network of people who love the work, while each funded project keeps its own stewards making the calls.

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. In a crisis, a clear chain of command can save lives, and a network that insists on consensus can fail people. And some things should stay purely networked: open knowledge, public conversation, the commons, where any central owner would be a point of capture.

The point is to notice which shape a situation needs, and to watch for hierarchy that has stopped being accountable or a network that has quietly grown an unaccountable center.

## The upward spiral

Hierarchy without networks becomes bureaucracy. Networks without hierarchy become drift.

Done well, each one feeds the other. Clear ownership gives a network the ability to act, and open connection keeps the owners honest and the structure alive.
