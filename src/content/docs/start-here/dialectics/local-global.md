## The tension

The local is a particular place and its people: their land, language, knowledge and ways of living. The global is the planet-spanning web of capital, information, trade and networks that connects everyone.

They look opposed. Local communities are rooted, specific and accountable, but they can be isolated, under-resourced and parochial. Global systems are powerful, connected and resource-rich, but they tend to flatten difference, extract value and set the terms from far away.

This connects closely to [Sovereignty x Interdependence](/start-here/dialectics/sovereignty-interdependence/) and to the picture in [Towards a More Sacred Economy](/start-here/dialectics/sacred-global-economy/#towards-a-more-sacred-economy). The question is how the global can serve the local rather than consume it. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/start-here/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: local.** Life is lived somewhere. Place-based communities hold knowledge, relationships and responsibilities that no global system can replace.
- **Antithesis: global.** We share one planet and one set of problems. Global networks move resources, ideas and solidarity to places that need them.
- **Synthesis: cosmolocal.** Global networks of local communities. Share knowledge, tools and capital globally, and make decisions, build and grow locally.

## What each side protects

| Pole   | What it protects                                 | What it becomes alone       |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Local  | Rootedness, specificity, accountability to place | Isolation and parochialism  |
| Global | Connection, resources, shared fate               | Homogenization and extraction |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume value flows only one way: from the local up to the global, as resources extracted and profits sent away.

But it can flow the other way too. Global capital and knowledge can move toward local communities and stay there, strengthening them on their own terms. "Light things global, heavy things local": ideas, designs and money travel, while land, food, governance and relationships stay rooted.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Global funding, local decisions.** Money can come from anywhere. What to do with it gets decided by the people who live there.
- **Shared knowledge, local adaptation.** Open tools and designs that each community shapes to its own place.
- **Shield first, then interface.** As in [Towards a More Sacred Economy](/start-here/dialectics/sacred-global-economy/#towards-a-more-sacred-economy), communities build their own economy before they meet the global one on their own terms.
- **Solidarity across places.** Local communities connecting to each other directly, not only through global intermediaries.

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. Some local things should stay local: sacred sites, community land, knowledge that isn't meant to travel. And some problems are irreducibly global, like climate or pandemics, and need coordination at that scale.

The point is to choose the scale deliberately rather than letting the bigger system decide by default.

## The upward spiral

Local without global becomes isolated. Global without local becomes extractive.

Done well, each one feeds the other. Strong local communities make the global network resilient and diverse, and a good global network gives local communities resources and allies.
