## What is an ontology?

An ontology is a picture of what exists and how it relates. It's the set of basic categories you use to make sense of the world: what's real, what matters, what counts as value, and how things connect to each other.

Everyone has one, whether they've written it down or not. Most of the time it's invisible, because it's the lens you look through rather than something you look at.

## Why it matters so much

An ontology informs everything downstream of it. What you believe exists shapes what you notice. What you notice shapes what you measure. What you measure shapes what you fund. And what you fund shapes the world.

You can see this in economics. If your ontology says that value is whatever has a price, you build markets that are blind to relationships, the living world and everything else that can't be priced, and then you're surprised when those things get used up. If your ontology says that people are isolated individuals maximizing their own gain, you design institutions that make that true.

Change the ontology, and different things become visible, different mechanisms make sense, and a different economy becomes possible.

## Thousands of mimetic tribes, talking past each other

There are thousands of mimetic tribes out there: groups held together by a shared story about what's real, what's good and who the enemy is. Each one has its own ontology, its own vocabulary, and its own sense of what's obvious.

What you see on social media these days is mostly these tribes talking past each other. The same word means different things to different groups. An argument that's airtight inside one ontology sounds absurd, or even dangerous, inside another. People aren't only disagreeing about answers. They're working from different pictures of what exists, so they can't agree on what the question is.

That raises a hard question: how can we understand the points other people are making when we don't have an ontological transmission protocol, and most of us can't think from inside another ontology?

Our starting point for this is [Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/).

## Start with ontology

Our ontology informs everything we do: the mission, the roadmap, the mechanisms we experiment with, the record types our apps read and write, and how we decide what counts as success. If we skip this step, we inherit the ontology of the economy we're trying to move beyond, and we end up rebuilding it with better tools.

So we try to make ours explicit. The pages in this section lay it out:

- **[Mission: Fund What Matters](/ontology/fund-what-matters/)**: what we're for.
- **[Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/)**: how we work with opposites like love and money, rather than choosing between them.
- **[Dialectic: Love x Funding](/ontology/dialectic/)** and the dialectics that follow: the tensions at the heart of a new economy, and what each side protects.
- **[The Upward Spiral](/ontology/upward-spiral/)**: how those tensions, held well, feed each other and move upward.

It's a living ontology. We expect it to change as we learn, and we'd rather argue about it in the open than leave it unexamined.
