## The tension

Openness means anyone can join, contribute, use and build on what's there. Boundaries mean some things are protected: who belongs, what's allowed, what can be taken out.

They look opposed. Openness is generous, inclusive and fast-growing, but open systems can be captured, overrun or extracted from by people who take without giving. Boundaries protect a community's values and resources, but they can become walls that exclude, stagnate and turn into gatekeeping.

The question is how to stay open without being captured. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: openness.** Knowledge, tools and communities grow when anyone can take part. Open systems invite contribution from places nobody expected.
- **Antithesis: boundaries.** Anything valuable will attract people who want to take it. Without boundaries, a commons gets enclosed, polluted or turned against its own purpose.
- **Synthesis: a governed commons.** Open by default, bounded where it counts. Clear rules, set by the community, about what's shared, who decides, and what it takes to belong.

## What each side protects

| Pole       | What it protects                               | What it becomes alone       |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Openness   | Inclusion, generosity, growth                  | Capture and extraction      |
| Boundaries | Integrity, safety, a community's values        | Gatekeeping and stagnation  |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume a boundary has to be a wall, a hard line between insiders and outsiders.

But boundaries can be membranes. A cell membrane lets nutrients in and keeps toxins out. Elinor Ostrom's research on commons showed that the ones that last have clear boundaries and rules, set and enforced by the people who use them. The boundary is what lets the commons stay open to the people it serves.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Open source with stewards.** Anyone can use and contribute, and a community decides what gets merged.
- **Licenses that protect the commons.** Share-alike and similar terms that keep shared work from being enclosed.
- **Clear norms for belonging.** Easy to join, with expectations about how you show up.
- **Protection from capture.** Rules that stop any one funder, company or faction from taking over.

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. Some things should be fully open, like public knowledge and basic human rights. And some things need firm boundaries: sacred knowledge that isn't meant to be shared, a community's land, a safe space for people who've been harmed.

The point is to choose the boundary on purpose, not to have it set by whoever has the most power.

## The upward spiral

Openness without boundaries gets captured. Boundaries without openness become walls.

Done well, each one feeds the other. Good boundaries make it safe to be open, and openness keeps the boundaries from hardening.
