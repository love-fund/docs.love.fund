## The tension

The modern global economy is the system most of the world runs on: markets, capital, trade, growth, and money as the common measure of value. A sacred economy is one where the economy serves what people hold sacred: relationships, the living world, community, and the things that can't be priced.

They look opposed. The global economy is powerful, productive and connected, but it tends to extract, flatten difference and turn everything into a commodity. A sacred economy is rooted, meaningful and regenerative, but on its own it can be small, under-resourced and vulnerable to being overrun.

This page looks at the tension between them, then at a picture of the journey from one to the other. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: the global economy.** It feeds billions, spreads tools and knowledge, and lets strangers cooperate at planetary scale. Pretending we can simply leave it is a fantasy.
- **Antithesis: the sacred economy.** Some things are worth more than any price: land, kin, culture, the living world. An economy that can't see them will consume them.
- **Synthesis: sacred economy, interfacing on its own terms.** Communities weave their own economy first, shielded from extraction. When they're ready, they meet the global economy deliberately, so that money is transformed into their values instead of their values being transformed by money.

## Towards a more sacred economy

We want to bring people from the modern global economy into a sacred economy.

### What the picture shows

The image reads left to right as a journey in three parts: where people start, a protected middle, and where they're headed.

### From: the modern global economy

On the left are rigid, parallel grey lines. That's the modern global economy: uniform, mechanical, and built to pull everything into the same channels.

Those lines bend toward the right and start to fray. The old system doesn't vanish; it reaches into the space beside it.

### The middle: finance as a shield

The yellow band in the middle is a protected space, and it has two stages.

**Initially, finance provides a shield.** Money keeps out extraction and dependency while a community is learning and weaving a sacred economy. In this stage, funding isn't there to plug people into the global economy. It buys them time and room to build something of their own.

**When you feel ready, you interface with the modern economy on your own terms.** The community chooses when to engage, and the goal of engaging is specific: so that money can be transformed into your value system, instead of your values being transformed by money.

The order matters. Shield first, then interface. Connect too early and the bigger system sets the terms.

### To: a sacred economy

On the right is the sacred economy: a soft, glowing field of color rather than a set of lines. Above it is a woven net, and each thread is one of the things it's made from:

- Dreams
- Community plans
- Knowledge of indigenous tools
- Allyship principles
- Knowledge of modern world tools
- Cosmologies of the Amazon
- Living world wisdom

Two details stand out. First, it's woven: these threads only become an economy when they're braided together. Second, modern world tools are one of the threads. A sacred economy doesn't reject the modern world; it takes what's useful and weaves it in alongside older knowledge.

### What it means for love.fund

This is a picture of what [Love x Fund](/ontology/dialectic/) is reaching for: money that serves what people love, rather than people serving money. It suggests a few things about how we fund:

- **Funding can be protective.** Sometimes the most useful thing money can do is give a community room to learn without having to sell out to survive.
- **Let communities set the pace.** "When you feel ready" is their call, not the funder's.
- **Translate money into values, not the other way round.** The point of engaging with the wider economy is to bring money into a community's value system.
- **Weave, don't replace.** Dreams, community plans, indigenous knowledge and modern tools all belong in the net.

## What each side protects

| Pole            | What it protects                                   | What it becomes alone        |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Global economy  | Productivity, connection, access to tools and capital | Extraction and commodification |
| Sacred economy  | Meaning, relationship, the living world            | Isolation and vulnerability  |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume contact always flows one way: the bigger system absorbs the smaller, and money always reshapes values in its own image.

But the direction depends on the terms. When a community engages from a position of strength, with its own economy woven and its own values clear, it can take what's useful from the global economy and bring it inside. In the [picture above](#towards-a-more-sacred-economy), modern world tools are one thread of the sacred economy's net, woven in alongside dreams, community plans, indigenous knowledge and living world wisdom.

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. Some things should stay outside the market entirely: sacred sites, ceremonies, knowledge that isn't meant to be sold. And sometimes a community needs to refuse a deal outright, because the terms would capture it.

The point is that the boundary is the community's to draw.

## The upward spiral

The global economy without the sacred becomes extraction. The sacred without any interface to the wider world becomes fragile.

Done well, each one feeds the other. A sacred economy gives money a compass, and the global economy gives the sacred the tools and reach to grow. This is what [Love x Fund](/ontology/dialectic/) is reaching for: money that serves what people love, rather than people serving money.
