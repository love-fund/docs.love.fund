## The tension

Scarcity is the fact that resources run out. There are only so many hours, so much energy, so much money and so much land. Abundance is the fact that some things grow when you share them: love, knowledge, trust, creativity.

They look opposed. A scarcity mindset is prudent and realistic, but it can turn into hoarding, fear and zero-sum competition. An abundance mindset is generous and hopeful, but it can turn into denial, waste and promises nobody can keep.

The [Love x Funding](/ontology/dialectic/) page says it plainly: love is infinite, resources are finite. The question is how to live with both. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: scarcity.** Resources are finite and choices have costs. Taking that seriously is what makes stewardship, budgets and care for the living world possible.
- **Antithesis: abundance.** The most important things aren't used up by sharing. A community that shares knowledge, trust and care becomes richer, not poorer.
- **Synthesis: regenerative abundance.** Treat finite resources with care, and use them to grow the things that multiply. Spend scarce money and time on what generates more love, more trust and more capacity.

## What each side protects

| Pole      | What it protects                              | What it becomes alone |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Scarcity  | Prudence, stewardship, honesty about limits   | Hoarding and fear     |
| Abundance | Generosity, hope, sharing                     | Denial and waste      |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume everything behaves the same way, either all scarce or all abundant.

But different resources behave differently. Atoms and energy are finite. Bits copy for free. Attention is fixed, but trust and creativity can grow with use. Money is somewhere in between: finite in any one moment, but it circulates, and where it flows shapes what grows. Much of our scarcity is also manufactured, created by how things are owned and distributed rather than by how much there is.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Know which resource you're dealing with.** The [six-part economy](/ontology/dialectic/#a-six-part-economy) (atoms, energy, bits, currency, attention, creativity) don't all run out the same way.
- **Spend scarce things on abundant ones.** Put limited money toward the commons, open knowledge and relationships that keep producing.
- **Share what doesn't diminish.** Open source, open research, shared tools and stories.
- **Watch for artificial scarcity.** Notice when "there isn't enough" really means "it's being held back."

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. Some limits are real and hard, like a planet's carbon budget or a project's last month of runway, and talk of abundance there can be dangerous. And some abundance shouldn't be rationed: kindness, dignity and welcome shouldn't be treated like they're in short supply.

The point is to be honest about which situation you're in.

## The upward spiral

Scarcity without abundance becomes fear. Abundance without scarcity becomes waste.

Done well, each one feeds the other. Care with limited resources lets abundance grow, and growing abundance makes the limits easier to live with.
