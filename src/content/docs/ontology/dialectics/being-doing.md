## The tension

Being is the inner life: presence, practice, clarity about what you love. Metta, meditation, prayer and reflection all live here. Doing is the outer life: action, building, funding, organizing, impact in the world.

They look opposed. Inner work is grounding and wise, but it can turn into withdrawal, spiritual bypassing or navel-gazing while the world burns. Outer work is effective and urgent, but it can turn into burnout, busyness and action disconnected from any real love.

The [Love x Funding](/ontology/dialectic/) page has a personal side (values, scorecards, dharma) and a systems side (money that flows like metta). This dialectic is about how they connect. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: being.** Change starts inside. Without clarity about what you love, you'll pour your time and money into whatever pulls at you loudest.
- **Antithesis: doing.** Love that never acts stays an idea. The world changes through what people actually do with their hours, attention and money.
- **Synthesis: dharma.** Action that comes from presence. Your inner practice tells you what you love, and your outer life is how that love takes form.

## What each side protects

| Pole  | What it protects                           | What it becomes alone          |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Being | Clarity, presence, rootedness in love      | Withdrawal and bypassing       |
| Doing | Impact, courage, love made real            | Burnout and empty busyness     |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume time spent on inner work is time taken from action, and the reverse.

But they're one loop. Practice clarifies what you love. Action tests it in the world. Reflection on that action deepens the practice. Contemplative traditions have long said that action itself can be a practice, done with full attention and without clinging to results.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Values before spending.** Get clear on what you love before deciding where your time and money go.
- **The personal scorecard.** Compare what you say you love with where your calendar and bank statement show you've actually been.
- **Rest as part of the work.** Sustainable action needs time to recover and reflect.
- **Fund from love, not guilt.** Giving that comes from care lasts longer than giving that comes from pressure.

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. Sometimes you need to stop doing and simply be: in grief, in burnout, in a season of listening. And sometimes you need to act before you've figured everything out, because someone needs help now.

The point is to notice which one you've been neglecting.

## The upward spiral

Being without doing becomes withdrawal. Doing without being becomes burnout.

Done well, each one feeds the other. Presence makes action wiser, and action gives presence something real to hold. Enough people living this way is how changed people change a society.
