title: Competition x Cooperation
description: Competition pushes quality up and cooperation builds what nobody can build alone. Coopetition points the race at the commons.

## The tension

Competition is people or projects trying to outdo each other: better work, a better price, more of the prize. Cooperation is people working together toward something they share, pooling effort so everyone ends up with more.

They look opposed. Competition sharpens people and weeds out what doesn't work, but it can turn into a race to the bottom where everyone cuts corners to win. Cooperation builds trust and shared infrastructure, but it can go soft, protect the people inside it and stop asking whether the work is any good.

The question is whether you can set up the game so that competing makes the shared pie bigger instead of carving it up. (For the general method, see [Beyond Dualism](/ontology/beyond-dualism/).)

## The dialectic

- **Thesis: competition.** Rivalry makes people better. It rewards what works, exposes what doesn't, and keeps any one group from getting comfortable with power it hasn't earned.
- **Antithesis: cooperation.** The things that matter most are held in common: roads, knowledge, ecosystems, open source code. Nobody wins them alone, and a fight over them usually leaves everyone worse off.
- **Synthesis: coopetition.** Compete to create more value for the commons. Projects still race to be the best, but the prize goes to whoever helps the shared thing most, and what each one builds stays open for the rest to use.

## What each side protects

| Pole        | What it protects                           | What it becomes alone       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Competition | Excellence, accountability, choice         | Race to the bottom          |
| Cooperation | Trust, shared infrastructure, resilience   | Complacency and closed circles |

A good synthesis keeps the left column of both rows and avoids the right column of both.

## The assumption to question

The two look incompatible because we assume competition is zero-sum: if you win, I lose, so there's a fixed prize and the only question is who gets it.

That depends on the rules. When the prize is a share of a fixed market, competing means taking from each other. When the prize is a reward for adding to something shared, like a funding round that backs the projects that help the most people, competing means adding more. The rules decide which game people are playing, and the rules can be designed.

## What it looks like in practice

- **Funding rounds as friendly races.** Projects compete for a matching pool by showing how many people they help, and the work they produce stays open. [Quadratic funding](https://www.wtfisqf.com/) is built this way: the winners are the projects with the broadest support.
- **Open source.** Companies compete on products and still maintain the same shared libraries, because everyone gains when the base layer is good.
- **Bounties and challenges.** A clear problem, a public prize and many teams trying. Even the teams that lose leave behind ideas and code others can use.
- **Rival communities, shared commons.** Local groups can compete to run the best events or programs and still share playbooks, venues and contacts.

## When to hold them apart

Not every case wants a synthesis. Some things should never be a competition: care for people in crisis, access to basic needs, anything where losing means getting hurt. And some situations need real rivalry with no shared prize, like an election or a market where cooperation between sellers would just be a cartel.

The point is to know which game you're in, and to notice when "collaboration" is really collusion or when "competition" is really extraction.

## The upward spiral

Competition without cooperation becomes a race to the bottom. Cooperation without competition becomes a closed circle.

Done well, each one feeds the other. Healthy rivalry raises the bar for the commons, and a strong commons gives everyone a better place to compete from.
