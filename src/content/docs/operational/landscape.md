<details class="tldr" open>
<summary>TL;DR</summary>

- The market splits into three groups: mainstream platforms, crypto-native and public-goods platforms, and relational and community finance.
- love.fund's 10% revenue target sits at the top of the market. Most big donation platforms charge creators no platform fee.
- Creators and donors are fairly well served. Sponsors, the people who want to fund a cause or match others, are the least served.

</details>

This page is a snapshot from research done on November 4, 2026. Figures are what each platform published at the time, and they change. Fee pages were read on 2026-11-04 unless another date is given. Where we couldn't find a primary source, we left the figure out. Lines marked "Our read" are our interpretation, not facts. None of this is legal or financial advice.

## 1. Mainstream platforms

**GoFundMe.** Free to start. One transaction fee of 2.9% + $0.30 per donation, plus optional donor tips. Recurring donations add a 5% fee paid by the supporter ([pricing](https://www.gofundme.com/pricing), read 2026-11-04; the page we fetched showed "GST Included", so the US rate still needs confirming). Nearly 80M donations and 72M+ shares in 2025, and more than $40B raised since 2010 ([Year in Help](https://www.gofundme.com/c/newsroom/2025-year-in-help-report), Dec 2025). Its Giving Funds DAF charges no admin fee ([pricing](https://www.gofundme.com/pricing), read 2026-11-04). It's built around one fundraiser at a time.

**Kickstarter.** 5% of funds raised, only if a project is funded, plus 3% + $0.30 processing per pledge ([fees](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/fees), read 2026-11-04). Backers buy rewards, so these are not donations. Kickstarter called 2025 its "biggest year to date", and its curated Open Calls had close to an 80% success rate ([2025 review](https://updates.kickstarter.com/a-year-in-review-2025-kickstarter-highlights), Dec 2025).

**Patreon.** Creators who publish after August 4, 2025 pay a 10% platform fee plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing on card payments over $3 ([creator fees](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/11111747095181-Creator-fees-overview), read 2026-11-04). More than $10B paid to creators since 2013 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/05/patreon-10-billion-creator-economy-ai), Aug 2025). Patronage is of one creator at a time.

**Every.org.** No platform fee. ACH, stock, DAF and IRA gifts are free; cards cost 2.2% + $0.30; optional tips cover costs ([pricing](https://www.every.org/pricing), read 2026-11-04). It's the closest mainstream analogue to a multi-nonprofit fund, for US 501(c)(3)s.

**Daffy.** A DAF with flat monthly tiers from free (balances up to $100) to $40/mo, and no asset-based fee ([membership](https://www.daffy.org/membership), read 2026-11-04). 15,700+ accounts and $125M+ donated in 2025 ([2025 review](https://www.daffy.org/resources/year-in-review-2025)). Strong on custody and tax, lighter on helping donors decide what to fund.

**Givebutter.** For nonprofits. With tips on, no platform or processing fee to the nonprofit. With tips off, 3% plus 2.9% + $0.30 on cards ([pricing](https://givebutter.com/pricing), read 2026-11-04). "Over $5B" powered ([blog](https://givebutter.com/blog/givebutter-5b-donation-milestone), Mar 2025).

**Open Collective.** Since March 2026, Collectives use the platform free with optional tips, or opt for a 5% fee ([pricing](https://opencollective.com/pricing), read 2026-11-04). Hosts add their own fee; Open Source Collective charges 10% of funds raised ([OSC](https://opencollective.com/osc-host-application), read 2026-11-04). Open Collective Foundation dissolved at the end of 2024 ([announcement](https://opencollective.com/foundation/updates/announcement-we-are-dissolving-open-collective-foundation-at-the-end-of-this-year)), and more than 600 projects holding $18M had to find new hosts ([blog](https://blog.opencollective.com/fiscal-sponsors-we-need-you)).

**GlobalGiving.** A nonprofit support fee of 5–12% plus 3% processing, so 8–15% in total ([fees](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/fee), read 2026-11-04). More than $1.1B raised since 2002 ([about](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus), read 2026-11-04). It shows a fee near 10% is accepted when it pays for vetting and amplification.

**Fidelity Charitable and DAFs.** 0.60% a year or $100, whichever is greater, and "typically… about 1%" of the balance in total ([costs](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/giving-account/what-it-costs.html), read 2026-11-04). Fidelity Charitable granted $18.3B in 2025 ([2026 Giving Report](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/insights/2026-giving-report.html)). Across the US, DAFs took in $90.57B and granted $64.60B in FY2024 ([DAF Research Collaborative](https://www.dafresearchcollaborative.org/research/annual-daf-report), 2025 report).

## 2. Crypto-native and public-goods platforms

**Gitcoin.** love.fund's incubator (see [Ecosystem](/reference/ecosystem/)). $60M+ distributed over 230+ rounds ([impact](https://gitcoin.co/impact), read 2026-11-04). GG24, in October 2025, distributed about $1.8M ([GG24](https://gitcoin.co/campaigns/gitcoin-grants-24-gg24)). We found no published platform fee.

**Giveth.** "Does not charge fees to givers or makers"; donors pay only network gas ([FAQ](https://docs.giveth.io/faq), read 2026-11-04). $7.2M+ raised for 7,367 projects ([giveth.io](https://www.giveth.io), read 2026-11-04).

**Octant.** Funds public goods from the staking yield on 100,000 ETH staked by the Golem Foundation; 2,340+ ETH to 80+ projects since August 2023 ([Gitcoin apps page](https://gitcoin.co/apps/octant), undated). This model works for treasury holders.

**Endaoment.** A crypto-friendly DAF: 0.5% on donations in (tiered down for large gifts) and 1.0% on grants out, or 1.5% for direct donations ([fee schedule](https://docs.endaoment.org/donors/resources/fee-schedule), read 2026-11-04).

**Juicebox and Drips.** Treasury and streaming tools for projects and DAOs. Juicebox charges 2.5% on payouts to wallets ([docs](https://docs.juicebox.money/user/project), read 2026-11-04). The Drips protocol charges no fee beyond gas ([FAQ](https://docs.drips.network/faq), read 2026-11-04).

**Hypercerts.** Impact-claim infrastructure, not a marketplace. It moved to ATProto in 2025 and is focused on real-world pilots in 2026 ([blog](https://hypercerts.org/blog/3mb6z2bku2c2z), Dec 2025).

**Optimism Retro Funding.** 81.4M OP committed so far, with 777.6M OP of its allocation preserved ([budget update](https://gov.optimism.io/t/collective-year-4-budget-update-and-year-5-budget-outlook/10796)). Retro Funding "will not run for at least 12 months" ([Season 9 guide](https://gov.optimism.io/t/guide-to-season-9/10529), 2026).

**Artizen.** Creative projects funded by $10 "Artifacts" and sponsor match pools, with $2.2M raised ([Gitcoin apps page](https://gitcoin.co/apps/artizen-fund), undated). Artizen ceased operations in October 2026, and its matching funds will not be paid out ([Bread Cooperative](https://paragraph.com/@breadcoop/artizen-shutdown-what-we-know), Oct 2026).

## 3. Relational and community finance

**Mutual aid.** "At least 800 mutual aid networks" operate in the US ([NEA](https://www.nea.org/nea-today/all-news-articles/mutual-aid-act-resistance-and-community-support), undated). Many run on general tools like Venmo, PayPal and GoFundMe. A Venmo charity profile pays 1.9% + $0.10 per donation ([Venmo](https://help.venmo.com/cs/articles/charity-profile-donation-fees-vhel159), read 2026-11-04). There's no purpose-built tool for pooled neighbourhood funds.

**Savings circles (ROSCAs).** MoneyFellows in Egypt charges admin fees that "start at 16% and progressively decrease to 0% for later slots" ([how it works](https://moneyfellows.com/en-us/how-it-works), read 2026-11-04), and has 8.5M+ users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/04/moneyfellows-raises-13m-to-take-its-group-savings-model-outside-egypt), May 2025).

**Sarafu Network (Kenya).** Community vouchers and "commitment pools": 251,449 peer-to-peer exchanges among 4,476 users between July 2023 and July 2025, and 95% of 855 surveyed users called it important to their household economy ([2025 study](https://grassecon.substack.com/p/2025-sarafu-network-study-results)).

**Participatory budgeting.** Decidim runs 574 instances in 36 countries ([used by](https://decidim.org/usedby), read 2026-11-04). NYC's The People's Money let residents allocate $4M in 2025 ([NYC](https://www.nyc.gov/site/civicengagement/news/the-peoples-money-returns-a-third-time-2025.page)), with 131,527 voters ([results](https://www.participate.nyc.gov/processes/Citywidepb2024/f/71)). The money is public and the process runs once a year.

Our read:

- love.fund targets 10% of platform activity as revenue, never charged to small donors (rules 8 and 20 on the [Narrow Aperture Path](/roadmap/narrow-aperture-path/)). That's the top of the market. Only Patreon, Open Source Collective and GlobalGiving charge that much.
- GoFundMe, Every.org and Givebutter (tips on) charge creators no platform fee.
- Card processing alone is 5–6% of a $10 gift. Keeping small gifts whole needs cheaper rails (ACH or stablecoins) or someone else covering processing.

## Who is least served: sponsors

- **Creators** are well served, with polished tools and platform fees from 0% to 10%.
- **Donors** are reasonably served. Small gifts are fee-free on several platforms, and DAFs now start at a few dollars a month.
- **Sponsors**, the people and institutions who want to fund a cause or match others, have fewer venues than a few years ago. Optimism paused Retro Funding. Artizen closed with match funding unpaid. Open Collective Foundation dissolved. Meanwhile US DAFs took in about $26B more than they granted in FY2024 (sources above).
- **Relational communities** (mutual aid, local pools) come second. There's proven demand for group money, but little purpose-built infrastructure.

Our read: sponsor money exists at scale. What's missing is a trusted, mainstream place to put matching and curated funds where small donors can join in.

## What this means for love.fund

These are directions, not commitments.

1. **Earn the 10% from sponsors and services, not from small gifts.** Keep small gifts free of platform fees, as the market does. Take revenue from sponsor-side work: matching pools, fund management, curation and reporting. GlobalGiving and Open Source Collective show that around 10% is accepted when it buys vetting and amplification.
2. **Make "the match is real" a feature.** Hold sponsor matches in escrow before a fund opens, show the reserve, and publish wind-down terms up front. This ties to rule 10: settle the legal structure first. Reliability matters more than mechanism novelty.
3. **Meet money and sharing where they already are.** Accept DAF grants without a fee. Offer DAF holders curated, forkable funds, since they've already set money aside. Make funds as easy to share as a single fundraiser. For communities, "a fund" can also mean a pooled local pot, which fits the [Techne](/reference/ecosystem/) direction.
